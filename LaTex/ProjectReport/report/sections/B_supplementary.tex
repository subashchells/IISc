\subsection{The four adversarial scenarios}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{l X}
\toprule
\textbf{Scenario} & \textbf{What it breaks} \\
\midrule
\code{all-critical} (20p, 5w)  &
  Every priority key is identical $\Rightarrow$ Priority degenerates
  to the tie-hash. \\
\code{long-chain} (1p, 5w)     &
  25 strictly serial tasks $\Rightarrow$ no parallelism; matcher
  choice can't help. \\
\code{single-role-bottleneck} (15p, 5w) &
  Only one PGY1 on the roster, all work needs PGY1. \\
\code{tight-shift} (12p, 4w)   &
  Shifts sized to the perfect-split share; zero slack. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

\subsection{Fair-mode does work --- here's a synthetic check}

A 3-nurse synthetic test where workers start at unequal load:
$W_1=200$~min, $W_2=100$~min, $W_3=0$~min already worked.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{l r r r r}
\toprule
\textbf{Strategy} & \textbf{$W_1$} & \textbf{$W_2$} & \textbf{$W_3$}
                  & \textbf{Makespan} \\
\midrule
Greedy            & 320 & 220 & 120 & baseline \\
Fair($\alpha=0$)  & 200 & 220 & 240 & baseline + 120~min \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent
Fair($\alpha\!=\!0$) actively pushes new work toward the under-loaded
$W_3$, at a 120-min makespan cost. The production ward just doesn't
exercise this case.

\subsection{Mini-glossary}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Acuity:} per-patient urgency tier (CRIT, HIGH,
        MED, LOW); smaller is more urgent.
  \item \textbf{Priority list:} list of ints compared left-to-right;
        smaller is more urgent on every dimension.
  \item \textbf{Makespan:} total schedule length.
  \item \textbf{Spread selection:} pick frontier points that span
        all three score axes, not just one corner.
\end{itemize}
