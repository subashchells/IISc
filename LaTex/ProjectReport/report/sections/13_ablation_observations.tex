\section{Ablation Studies and Observations}
\label{sec:ablation}

Each study holds two of the three policy axes fixed and varies one.

\subsection{Vary ordering only}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Hold matcher = Greedy. Compare FCFS vs.\ Priority.
  \item Makespan and total overtime: identical.
  \item Urgent-care metric: $-5{,}378$~min under Priority.
  \item Conclusion: priority redistributes who waits; it doesn't
        save total work.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Vary matcher only}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Hold ordering = FCFS. Compare Greedy vs.\ Hungarian.
  \item Makespan: $+90$~min under Hungarian; over-capacity tasks
        $+3$.
  \item Conclusion: ``optimal per tick'' costs the schedule.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Vary priority policy only}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Hold ordering + matcher fixed (Priority + Greedy).
  \item 7 named policies $\times$ 5 random seeds = 35 schedules.
  \item Best vs.\ worst: $2.9\times$ swing on the urgent-care
        metric for the same input.
  \item Two policies that agree on the dominant axis (acuity)
        differ by 408~min on dim-2 (age). A simple weighted-sum
        priority cannot distinguish them.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Vary fairness knob ($\alpha$) only}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Hold ordering + priority policy fixed.
  \item Throughput and worst overtime: identical across all $\alpha$.
  \item Within-role spread: rises monotonically as $\alpha$
        approaches 0.
  \item Greedy ($\alpha\!=\!1$) is the fairest configuration on
        this ward.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Four take-aways}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Ordering moves more than matching on a roster past
        capacity.
  \item ``Optimal per tick'' is the wrong invariant under burst
        arrivals with identical priorities.
  \item Fair-mode helps only when workers start unequal.
  \item No algorithm choice creates capacity --- 20{,}210~min of
        overtime is invariant.
\end{enumerate}
