\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

The system sits at the intersection of three classical sub-problems.

\subsection{Constrained job scheduling}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Schedule jobs on parallel machines, respecting a graph of
        ``A must finish before B'' rules.
  \item NP-hard in general; cheap list-scheduling heuristics get
        close to optimal in practice \citep{graham1969bounds, clrs}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Optimal one-to-one matching (Hungarian)}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Hungarian finds the cheapest
        one-to-one pairing in $O(n^3)$
        \citep{jonker1987shortest}.
  \item At our roster size ($\leq 10$) the cubic cost is
        negligible, so we use it as a ``per-tick optimum'' to
        compare against Greedy.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Picking from many candidates (multi-objective)}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Three smaller-is-better scores:
        urgent-care, makespan, worst overtime.
  \item Drop ``dominated'' candidates; from the rest, surface a
        spread of six picks for human review
        \citep{deb2002nsga, ehrgott2005multicriteria}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why a list of integers, not a single number}

\begin{itemize}
  \item A weighted-sum priority cannot pick between two schedules
        that agree on the dominant axis but differ on a tie-break
        \citep{esi2005}.
  \item A list of integers, compared left-to-right, can.
\end{itemize}
